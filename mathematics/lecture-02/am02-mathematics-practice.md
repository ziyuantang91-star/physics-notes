## AM02-N1

**Construct a first integral from a nonlinear equation**

**Source:** Original AI mathematics exercise supporting Unit 02.1.

**Prerequisites:** [AM02 Math - First Integrals and Phase Curves](am02-math-first-integrals-and-phase-curves.md)

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Developing (15–30 min).

Let dimensionless $q(\tau)$ satisfy $q''+3q+q^3=0$, where primes mean differentiation with respect to dimensionless time $\tau$. Derive a first integral without dividing by $q'$. Use $q(0)=1,q'(0)=0$ to find the speed at $q=0$ and all possible turning positions. Explain how the first direction of motion selects the speed sign.

## AM02-N2

**A phase ellipse is not a spatial orbit**

**Source:** Original AI mathematics exercise supporting Unit 02.1.

**Prerequisites:** [AM02 Math - First Integrals and Phase Curves](am02-math-first-integrals-and-phase-curves.md) · [AM01 Math 7 - Second Order Equations and Stability](../lecture-01/am01-math-7-second-order-equations-and-stability.md)

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Developing (15–30 min).

For dimensionless $q$ with $\ddot q+\kappa^2q=0$, $\kappa>0$, and $q(0)=A>0,\dot q(0)=0$, derive the phase curve and determine its direction of traversal. Identify where $dv/dq$ is undefined and show that the time solution is nevertheless smooth there. Use $v=\dot q$.

## AM02-N3

**An infinite-time approach with finite distance**

**Source:** Original AI mathematics exercise supporting Unit 02.1.

**Prerequisites:** [AM02 Math - First Integrals and Phase Curves](am02-math-first-integrals-and-phase-curves.md)

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Developing (15–30 min).

Let $\ddot q+\omega^2\sin q=0$ with $\omega>0$, $q(0)=0$, and $\dot q(0)=2\omega$. Derive the time needed to reach $q\in(0,\pi)$. Show how the time diverges as $q=\pi-\epsilon$ approaches $\pi$. Why is “finite angular distance” insufficient to imply finite travel time?

## AM02-N4

**Bound the nonlinear period without special functions**

**Source:** Original AI mathematics exercise supporting Unit 02.1.

**Prerequisites:** [AM02 Math - First Integrals and Phase Curves](am02-math-first-integrals-and-phase-curves.md)

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Developing (15–30 min).

A pendulum-form equation has libration amplitude $A=\pi/3$ and $\omega>0$. Starting from the first integral, derive the period integral in the variable $\psi$ defined by $\sin(q/2)=k\sin\psi$. Bound the period above and below using only the range of its integrand. Prove that the period tends to $2\pi/\omega$ as $A\to0$, and explain why this is not an exact amplitude-independent period.
