## AM01-M01

**Rolling, a spring and the feasibility of static friction**

**Source:** Original AI transfer problem combining P121–P131 straight rolling with generalized dynamics; rotational energy is the scoped supplement in Physics 7.

**Prerequisite concepts:** AM01 Physics 1 - Constraints and Integrability (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 4 - DAlembert and the Kinetic Energy Equations (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 7 - Atwood Machine (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending))

**Prerequisite tags:** #am01/physics-1 #am01/physics-4 #am01/physics-7

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Transfer; 45 minutes.

A rigid wheel of mass $m$, radius $R$ and fixed-axis moment of inertia $I>0$ rolls without slipping down a fixed incline of angle $0<\alpha<\pi/2$. Its center is attached to an ideal spring acting along the incline, with extension $x$ measured downhill from zero spring force and stiffness $k>0$. The center force produces no torque about the center. Use the rotational energy derived in Physics 7. Derive the acceleration and signed static friction, taking uphill friction as positive. State the local no-slip feasibility condition for coefficient $\mu_s$, and explain why static friction can be present without dissipating energy in this model.

## AM01-M02

**An expanding vertical hoop and actuator power**

**Source:** Original AI transfer problem applying moving holonomic constraints and virtual work from P097–P098 and P183–P310.

**Prerequisite concepts:** AM01 Physics 3 - Virtual Displacements and Ideal Constraints (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 4 - DAlembert and the Kinetic Energy Equations (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 5 - Lagrangians and Generalized Potentials (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending))

**Prerequisite tags:** #am01/physics-3 #am01/physics-4 #am01/physics-5

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Advanced transfer; 50 minutes.

A bead of mass $m$ is threaded on a smooth vertical circular hoop whose radius $R(t)>0$ is prescribed and whose center is fixed. Let $\mathbf r=(R\sin\theta,-R\cos\theta)$ with height upward and $\theta$ measured from downward vertical. Derive the angular equation and the signed outward radial reaction. Compute the reaction's actual power, prove the corresponding mechanical-energy balance, and compare physical energy with the generalized energy of the reduced one-coordinate Lagrangian. The hoop model permits either sign of the radial reaction.

## AM01-M04

**A sliding wedge, spring and conserved horizontal momentum**

**Source:** Original AI transfer problem combining generalized-coordinate coupling with the cyclic-coordinate consequence of the lecture equations.

**Prerequisite concepts:** AM01 Physics 2 - Generalized Coordinates and Velocities (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 4 - DAlembert and the Kinetic Energy Equations (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 5 - Lagrangians and Generalized Potentials (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · [AM01 Math 7 - Second Order Equations and Stability](../../mathematics/lecture-01/am01-math-7-second-order-equations-and-stability.md)

**Prerequisite tags:** #am01/physics-2 #am01/physics-4 #am01/physics-5 #am01/math-7

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Advanced transfer; 55 minutes.

A wedge of mass $M>0$ moves freely along a smooth horizontal floor. A bead of mass $m>0$ slides on its smooth straight incline of angle $0<\alpha<\pi/2$. A spring fixed to the wedge acts along the incline, with extension $s$ positive downhill from zero spring force and stiffness $k>0$. Let $X$ locate the wedge and write the bead's position as $(X+s\cos\alpha,-s\sin\alpha)$ up to fixed offsets. Derive both coupled equations, use horizontal momentum to reduce the $s$ motion, and find its equilibrium and oscillation frequency. Reconstruct $\dot X$ for general conserved momentum $P$, check the heavy-wedge limit, and state the normal-contact condition for a bead resting on the incline.

## AM01-M05

**Spherical pendulum: a cyclic coordinate and a conical motion**

**Source:** Original AI transfer problem; spherical geometry and the energy/cyclic-coordinate tools are supplementary applications, not claimed additional lecture sections.

**Prerequisite concepts:** AM01 Physics 2 - Generalized Coordinates and Velocities (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 5 - Lagrangians and Generalized Potentials (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · [AM01 Math 6 - Linear Algebra for Constraints](../../mathematics/lecture-01/am01-math-6-linear-algebra-for-constraints.md) · [AM01 Math 7 - Second Order Equations and Stability](../../mathematics/lecture-01/am01-math-7-second-order-equations-and-stability.md)

Use $\mathbf r=(\ell\sin\theta\cos\phi,\ell\sin\theta\sin\phi,-\ell\cos\theta)$ with $0<\theta<\pi$, gravity downward. Derive both equations and the conserved azimuthal momentum $J$. Reduce the polar equation using that constant and identify an effective potential. Find the condition for a nonvertical conical motion with constant $\theta_0$ and $\dot\phi=\Omega$, recover rod tension, and test polar stability at fixed $J$. Explain why holding $\dot\phi$ fixed during a free perturbation would be a different physical condition.

## AM01-M06

**A time-dependent velocity potential and the missing force term**

**Source:** Original AI transfer problem motivated by the generalized-potential discussion P337–P344; the electromagnetic component model is supplied, not claimed as lecture-derived.

**Prerequisite concepts:** AM01 Physics 5 - Lagrangians and Generalized Potentials (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 6 - Equivalent Lagrangians (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending))

**Prerequisite tags:** #am01/physics-5 #am01/physics-6

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Advanced transfer; 50 minutes.

Consider the planar Lagrangian
$$L=\tfrac12m(\dot x^2+\dot y^2)+\frac{eB(t)}2(x\dot y-y\dot x)-\tfrac12k(x^2+y^2),$$
where $B(t)$ is smooth and prescribed, $m,k>0$. Derive both equations, separating terms proportional to $B$ and $\dot B$. Find the physical energy $T+V$ and its rate. Then add the total derivative of $F=eB(t)xy/2$ and verify the resulting alternative Lagrangian gives the same equations. Explain why replacing a constant $B$ by $B(t)$ in the magnetic force alone misses part of this model.

## AM01-M09

**A time-dependent Lagrangian that deliberately adds damping**

**Source:** Original AI transfer problem connecting SC-10 and SC-11; an integrating-factor construction is supplementary.

**Prerequisite concepts:** AM01 Physics 5 - Lagrangians and Generalized Potentials (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 6 - Equivalent Lagrangians (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · [AM01 Math 7 - Second Order Equations and Stability](../../mathematics/lecture-01/am01-math-7-second-order-equations-and-stability.md)

**Prerequisite tags:** #am01/physics-5 #am01/physics-6 #am01/math-7

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Advanced transfer; 45 minutes.

The damped oscillator obeys $m\ddot q+b\dot q+kq=0$, with $m,b,k>0$. Let $L_0=m\dot q^2/2-kq^2/2$. Determine a constant $\gamma$ for which $L_d=e^{\gamma t}L_0$ gives this damped equation as a homogeneous Euler–Lagrange equation. Compare physical mechanical energy with $E_{L_d}$ and verify both rates. Explain why this construction neither makes a nonconstant scale an equivalence of the undamped system nor contradicts the limited no-go result for representing one-dimensional drag by a smooth generalized potential with the usual physical kinetic energy.

## AM01-M10

**A moving coordinate system without a moving physical support**

**Source:** Original AI transfer problem on point-coordinate transformations and energy conventions; supplementary interpretation of P173–P176 and P352–P367.

**Prerequisite concepts:** AM01 Physics 2 - Generalized Coordinates and Velocities (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 5 - Lagrangians and Generalized Potentials (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · AM01 Physics 6 - Equivalent Lagrangians (not included; [edition scope](../../SOURCES.md#pending)) · [AM01 Math 7 - Second Order Equations and Stability](../../mathematics/lecture-01/am01-math-7-second-order-equations-and-stability.md)

**Prerequisite tags:** #am01/physics-2 #am01/physics-5 #am01/physics-6 #am01/math-7

**Estimated difficulty/time:** Advanced transfer; 50 minutes.

A physical one-dimensional oscillator has fixed laboratory equilibrium $x=0$ and $L_x=m\dot x^2/2-kx^2/2$, with $m,k>0$. Introduce only a coordinate change $x=q+ut$, where $u$ is a constant speed; the spring support itself does not move. Construct the transformed Lagrangian, remove its removable terms using a specified total derivative, and derive the equation for $q$. Compare the reduced generalized energy with the conserved laboratory mechanical energy. Obtain $q(t)$ for $q(0)=q_0$, $\dot q(0)=v_0$, and explain why explicit time dependence here does not imply an actuator supplying energy.
