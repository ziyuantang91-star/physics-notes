## Hypothetical exercise cards

These are invented teaching inputs, not measurements, papers, simulations or research findings. Do not cite them as literature or enter them into the project's scientific evidence ledger.

| Card | What is supplied | What is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D | For one hypothetical device under one stated repeatable protocol, the electrical maximum-gap metric decreases after conditioning; a simultaneously measured mechanical loop is unchanged within the stated measurement resolution | Independent device replication; a uniquely isolating intervention; numerical resolution; environmental drift checks |
| E | For another hypothetical device under a different pressure range and loading rate, both electrical and mechanical loop measures decrease after conditioning | Matched protocol with D; comparable metric definitions; independent device replication; exclusion of instrument drift |

<a name="section-3"></a>

## Your tasks

1. Write a specific review question relevant to pressure-sensor hysteresis. State what comparison it requires and one boundary on its scope.
2. Build a two-row evidence table for D/E, distinguishing observation, possible explanation, missing information and comparison limits. Do not fill missing fields by guessing.
3. Write **150–220 words** explaining what the cards can and cannot support about a common mechanism. Treat them explicitly as hypothetical. Include a warrant linking an observation to your bounded conclusion.
4. Propose one discriminating measurement or controlled intervention. State the competing explanations and what differing result you would expect under each; identify one confounder that could defeat the test.
5. After feedback, revise your paragraph yourself and write a brief explanation of the most important reasoning change. An AI-written replacement does not count as your independent revision.

<a name="section-4"></a>

## Evaluation rubric

Use 0 = absent/incorrect, 1 = partly supported, 2 = explicit and defensible for each dimension. Scores are feedback aids, not automatic mastery or publication approval.

| Dimension | Evidence of a strong response |
| --- | --- |
| Question and claim scope | Specifies a testable comparison and stays within the available evidence |
| Traceability and provenance | Labels hypothetical inputs and preserves missing information |
| Comparability | Identifies protocol and metric differences before ranking or pooling |
| Observation versus interpretation | Distinguishes reported behavior, proposed explanation and own inference |
| Alternatives and discriminating test | Explains what the test separates and a relevant confounder |
| Independent revision | Explains a correction to reasoning rather than only changing style |
