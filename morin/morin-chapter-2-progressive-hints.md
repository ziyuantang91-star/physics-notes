## C02-M01 — An upright rod with a torsional spring

> **Hint 1**
> Gravity increases a small tilt from upright, while the spring opposes it. Compare their torques.

> **Hint 2**
> Linearize the net torque and use the supplied inertia for the oscillation frequency.

> **Hint 3**
> Near the threshold retain the cubic term; at the threshold inspect the leading nonzero potential term.

## C02-M02 — Resolve three-support indeterminacy with elasticity

> **Hint 1**
> Start with all three springs active and use the linear deflection formula.

> **Hint 2**
> Force and torque balance determine z and ψ; inspect the sign of every reaction as a grows.

> **Hint 3**
> If the left spring would pull, remove that contact and solve the two remaining reactions, then check the gap at the left spring.

## C02-M03 — Sliding or tipping on an accelerating platform

> **Hint 1**
> Include the specified fictitious force, or use ma in the inertial frame. State which route you take.

> **Hint 2**
> Take moments about the center of mass to locate the normal resultant.

> **Hint 3**
> Apply both the friction limit and the footprint constraint; the smaller acceleration threshold controls.

## C02-M04 — A capstan holding and lowering a load

> **Hint 1**
> For a stationary mass the load-side tension is its weight. Use the static ratio as a bound.

> **Hint 2**
> Check the rope tension on its high side as well as the operator limit.

> **Hint 3**
> In steady lowering the two rope ends move at the same speed in magnitude; compare gravitational power with power absorbed at the hand.

## C02-M05 — Infer a distributed load from two force sensors

> **Hint 1**
> Compute total force and moment directly by integration before replacing the load by a point resultant.

> **Hint 2**
> Use the total and moment to solve for the two reactions, then form combinations that eliminate β.

> **Hint 3**
> The resultant position is L times the fraction of total load read by the right sensor; differentiate that ratio.

## C02-M06 — A radial tether in circular orbital motion

> **Hint 1**
> Write one radial equation for each mass and give the internal force opposite signs.

> **Hint 2**
> Adding equations eliminates tension and determines the shared angular rate.

> **Hint 3**
> Substitute the resulting rate back into either equation; only then expand in ℓ/R.


<a name="section-8"></a>

## C02-M07 — A frictional loading path is not fixed by equilibrium alone

> **Hint 1**
> Set F=μmg. During sticking x stays fixed while the required friction varies between −F and +F.

> **Hint 2**
> On forward sliding and reverse sliding the friction signs are opposite. Find where reversal begins during unloading.

> **Hint 3**
> Add the distances traveled in both sliding directions; do not treat the residual extension as zero.


<a name="section-9"></a>

## C02-M08 — Infer a nonuniform hanging rope from tension data

> **Hint 1**
> A local rope element relates the derivative of tension to local weight per length.

> **Hint 2**
> Use the lower-end boundary tension for the point payload, then integrate density and its first moment.

> **Hint 3**
> To disprove uniqueness from top tension, compare a constant density with a linearly increasing density of the same total mass.


<a name="section-10"></a>

## C02-M09 — A ladder with uncertain position, angle and horizontal loading

> **Hint 1**
> Find the wall normal by taking torque about the foot; then find signed floor friction.

> **Hint 2**
> Express friction as A(a)cot(θ) minus a nonnegative multiple of H.

> **Hint 3**
> Find the smallest and largest possible signed friction over the independent input intervals, and test both against μ₀(W+P).

## C02-M10 — Stress in a uniformly rotating rod

> **Hint 1**
> Cut at r and isolate the portion from r to L. Its elements have different radial accelerations.

> **Hint 2**
> Integrate required inward force; the largest tension occurs at one endpoint.

> **Hint 3**
> Apply the stress limit after dividing by cross section, then integrate the speed-squared kinetic energy separately.
